Editorial standards

How we handle evidence

Trust is earned slowly. These standards explain how articles are researched, labelled, corrected and limited.

Our principles

  1. Evidence-informed explanationsClaims are based on the best available evidence, prioritising systematic reviews, guidelines and well-designed studies.
  2. Correlation is not causationAssociations from observational research are described as associations, not presented as proof of cause.
  3. Uncertainty is stated openlyWhere evidence is limited, mixed or still developing, articles say so.
  4. BalanceBenefits, limitations and risks are discussed together. Who an idea may not suit matters as much as who it may help.
  5. Respectful, accessible languageNo shaming, moralising about bodies or food, or unnecessary jargon.
  6. No miracle curesWe avoid fearmongering, exaggerated promises and products presented as solutions.

Evidence labels

Key claims in articles may carry one of three labels:

Well supported A substantial, consistent body of good-quality research, often reflected in systematic reviews or major guidelines.

Emerging / mixed Some supporting evidence, but limited, inconsistent, mainly observational, or not yet confirmed in relevant populations.

Uncertain Plausible ideas, mechanisms or common practice without strong direct evidence.

These labels are editorial judgements to help readers, not formal grading systems. They may change as evidence develops.
